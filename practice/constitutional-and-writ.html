Constitutional and writ matters

Challenging action by the State in the High Court or the Supreme Court, under Articles 226 and 32 of the Constitution.

General information, last reviewed 13 October 2026.

What this covers

  • Orders, notices and inaction of government departments, local bodies and other public authorities.
  • Claims that a fundamental right has been violated, including the rights to equality (Article 14), to freedom (Article 19) and to life and personal liberty (Article 21).
  • Challenges to the validity of rules, notifications and executive orders.
  • Habeas corpus, where a person is said to be detained unlawfully.
  • Public interest litigation, where a petitioner raises a matter of wider public concern.

Where these matters are heard

Under Article 226, the High Court can issue writs for the enforcement of fundamental rights and for any other legal right. The Allahabad High Court sits at Prayagraj and at Lucknow, and the Lucknow Bench hears matters arising from the districts assigned to it.

Under Article 32, a person can go directly to the Supreme Court of India for the enforcement of fundamental rights. Appeals from High Courts, including by special leave under Article 136, also lie to the Supreme Court.

The five classic writs are habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. A court can also pass any other direction that suits the case.

What courts look at first

  • Alternative remedy. If the law gives an effective appeal or tribunal, the High Court may ask the petitioner to use it first. This is a rule of discretion, not an absolute bar.
  • Delay. No fixed limitation period applies to a writ petition, but unexplained delay can lead a court to refuse relief.
  • Who is the respondent. Writs are aimed at the State and at bodies that perform public functions. A purely private dispute is ordinarily not a matter for a writ.

Papers that usually matter

  • The order or letter being challenged, with the date it was received.
  • Any representation already sent and any reply.
  • Documents that show the right claimed: an appointment letter, a licence, an allotment, a rule or notification.
  • A short chronology of events in date order.

Common questions

Is there a fixed time limit for a writ petition?

No fixed period is prescribed. Courts expect a petition to be filed promptly, and a long, unexplained delay can be a reason to refuse relief.

Can a writ be filed against a private company?

Ordinarily not for a private dispute. Writs are directed at the State and at bodies that perform public functions. Whether a particular body qualifies depends on its facts.

What is the difference between Article 226 and Article 32?

Article 32 gives a direct route to the Supreme Court for fundamental rights. Article 226 gives access to the High Court for fundamental rights and for other legal rights, so its reach is wider.
