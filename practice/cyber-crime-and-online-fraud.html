Cyber crime and online fraud

Online fraud, impersonation, account takeover and other offences committed through computers and phones, from the first report to the court case.

General information, last reviewed 14 October 2026.

What this covers

  • UPI, card and net-banking fraud, including phishing and calls that ask for an OTP.
  • Fake investment schemes, job offers and online shops.
  • Identity theft and impersonation.
  • Unauthorised access to accounts and devices.
  • Online harassment, defamation and the sharing of private images.

What to do in the first hours

  1. Tell the bank or wallet provider at once, by phone and then in writing, and ask them to block the card, account or UPI handle.
  2. Report the fraud on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call the helpline, 1930. Quick reports give the system a better chance to hold the money.
  3. Keep the evidence: screenshots, transaction IDs and UTR numbers, phone numbers, links, emails with their full headers, and bank statements. Do not delete chats.
  4. Give a written complaint to the police station or the cyber crime police station, and keep the acknowledgement number.

Laws that commonly apply

The Information Technology Act, 2000 covers unauthorised access and damage to computers (Section 43), identity theft (Section 66C) and cheating by personation using a computer resource (Section 66D). The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 covers cheating (Section 318) and cheating by personation (Section 319). The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 governs the procedure, including how information about an offence is recorded.

The Reserve Bank of India's 2017 framework on unauthorised electronic banking transactions ties a customer's liability to who was at fault and how quickly the transaction was reported. The bank's current policy should be checked.

Where these matters are heard

  • The police and the Magistrate's court, for criminal proceedings.
  • The adjudicating officer under Section 46 of the Information Technology Act, for compensation claims up to ₹5 crore. Larger claims go to the civil court.
  • The banking ombudsman and consumer commissions, for disputes with banks and service providers.
  • The High Court, for bail, quashing of proceedings and writs.

Common questions

How quickly should a fraud be reported?

Immediately. Banks and the 1930 helpline can sometimes place a hold on funds if they are told quickly.

Do I need a lawyer to file a complaint?

No. A complaint can be made on the portal or at a police station without one.

Can I get my money back?

It depends on how fast the fraud was reported, where the money moved and the bank's policy. No outcome can be promised.
