Property and real estate

Title, possession, tenancy, development authority matters and disputes between buyers and builders, in Lucknow and across Uttar Pradesh.

General information, last reviewed 13 October 2026.

What this covers

  • Checking title before a purchase, and drafting and registering sale deeds, gift deeds and wills.
  • Suits for possession, partition, injunction and specific performance.
  • Tenancy and eviction, including matters under the Uttar Pradesh Urban Buildings (Regulation of Letting, Rent and Eviction) Act, 1972.
  • Disputes between home buyers and builders under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016.
  • Allotment, cancellation, map sanction and demolition matters involving development authorities.
  • Revenue records and agricultural land, including proceedings before revenue courts under the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006.

Where these matters are heard

  • Civil courts, for title, possession, partition, injunction and specific performance.
  • The Uttar Pradesh Real Estate Regulatory Authority and its Appellate Tribunal, for registered projects.
  • Consumer commissions, for deficiency in service by a builder, under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
  • The High Court, by writ, for action by development authorities and other public bodies.
  • Revenue courts, for mutation and other revenue disputes.

Time limits to keep in mind

Under the Limitation Act, 1963, a suit for possession of immovable property based on title generally has to be filed within twelve years from when the possession of the other side became adverse. A suit for specific performance of a contract generally has to be filed within three years of the date fixed for performance, or of when the plaintiff learned that performance was refused. Which rule applies depends on the facts, so dates should be checked early.

Two points that are often misunderstood

  • A power of attorney lets someone act for the owner. It does not by itself transfer ownership. A sale of immovable property worth ₹100 or more needs a registered deed (Section 54, Transfer of Property Act, 1882), and the Supreme Court said in Suraj Lamps and Industries v. State of Haryana (2011) that title does not pass by a general power of attorney, an agreement to sell or a will alone.
  • Mutation in the revenue records shows who pays the land revenue or tax. It is not, on its own, proof of ownership.

Papers that usually matter

  • The chain of title: every earlier registered deed, in order.
  • An encumbrance certificate and the revenue records (khatauni or khasra).
  • Tax receipts, the sanctioned map and any allotment letter.
  • For a flat or plot in a project: the builder-buyer agreement, the RERA registration number, payment receipts and correspondence.

Common questions

Does a power of attorney transfer ownership?

No. It authorises another person to act for the owner. Ownership of immovable property passes through a registered conveyance, such as a sale deed.

Where can a flat buyer complain about delay?

A buyer in a registered project can complain to the Uttar Pradesh RERA. A consumer commission is another route. Which one suits a case depends on the relief sought and the facts.

Is a mutation entry proof of ownership?

No. It records who is in the revenue records for tax purposes. Title is decided on the documents and the law.
