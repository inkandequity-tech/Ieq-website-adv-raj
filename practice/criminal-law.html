Criminal law

Criminal complaints, bail, trials and appeals under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam.

General information, last reviewed 14 October 2026.

What this covers

  • Complaints and information about cognizable offences, and remedies where a police report is not recorded.
  • Regular and anticipatory bail, and bail applications in the courts of session and the High Court.
  • Trials, evidence and procedural applications.
  • Appeals, revisions and applications to quash proceedings where the law allows.
  • Compounding and settlement where permitted.

Governing law

Since 1 July 2024, criminal matters are governed by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (offences and punishments), the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (procedure) and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (evidence). The transition and savings provisions must be checked for the date of the offence and the stage of the case before any earlier law is relied on.

Papers that usually matter

  • The FIR or complaint, and the case diary or charge sheet where available.
  • Arrest and remand papers, and bail orders.
  • Medical and other reports, and the list of witnesses.
  • Proof of residence and surety documents for bail applications.

Common questions

Can an FIR be registered at any police station?

A complaint about a cognizable offence is ordinarily made to the police, and the procedure for recording it is set out in the BNSS. Remedies exist if the police do not act.

What is the difference between regular and anticipatory bail?

Regular bail is sought after arrest. Anticipatory bail is sought before arrest, where a person apprehends arrest. Each depends on the offence and the facts.
