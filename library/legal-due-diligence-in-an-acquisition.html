Legal Due Diligence in an Acquisition

What does legal due diligence cover in a company acquisition and how do findings change the deal?

Illustrative scenario. This is a hypothetical illustrative scenario prepared for general legal information. It does not describe an actual client matter or guarantee any particular outcome. The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

A fictional buyer is acquiring all shares of a fictional software company. Diligence finds: a former employee has raised a claim for dues and wrongful termination; some code contains components under licences with source-code disclosure conditions; several developers were contractors with no written IP assignment; and privacy practices are informal.

IIThe practical problem

Understanding what the risks mean for price and terms, and how to protect the buyer through the agreement.

IIIIssues

  1. IP ownership and chain of title.
  2. Open-source licence compliance.
  3. Employment claims.
  4. Data protection readiness.
  5. Contractual and regulatory exposure.
  6. How to allocate risk: price, indemnity, escrow, conditions.
  7. Limits of legal diligence.

IVLegal framework

  • Copyright Act, 1957: verify first ownership under Section 17 and the applicable employment/commission exceptions; assignments must satisfy the Act's formalities, including Section 19 where applicable. Contractor-created code may remain with the contractor absent an effective assignment, but assess the actual contract, authorship, employment status, commissioned-work provisions and licence terms.
  • Open-source compliance depends on the specific licence, how the component is used/distributed and the code architecture. A licence does not invariably require disclosure of an entire proprietary codebase; obtain a software bill of materials and qualified technical and legal review.
  • Employment: verify commencement notifications, applicable rules and transitional provisions for each labour code and the worker's/employee's status. Assess contractual restrictions under current law, including the limits on post-termination restraints.
  • Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Rules, 2025: commencement is phased. Check the official timeline for each relevant obligation at the transaction signing/closing date. Also assess the IT Act framework and other sectoral data/security requirements that remain applicable.
  • Companies Act, 2013 for share transfers; FEMA if the buyer is foreign; Competition Act thresholds.
  • Stamp duty on share transfer under the Indian Stamp Act as applicable.
  • Transaction documents: share purchase agreement, disclosure letter, warranties, indemnities, escrow, conditions precedent, covenants.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Agree scope and limits of the diligence, including that technical, tax and financial reviews need specialists.
  2. Review corporate records, contracts, IP assignments, employment files, litigation and compliance.
  3. Prepare a report with issues ranked by seriousness and with proposed actions.
  4. Fix what can be fixed before signing: obtain confirmatory IP assignments from contractors, remediate licence issues, settle the employee claim.
  5. Reflect remaining risks in the agreement: specific indemnities, price adjustment, escrow, conditions precedent and covenants.
  6. Plan post-closing integration and compliance.
  7. Maintain confidentiality in the data room.

VIDocuments and evidence

Constitutional documents and registers; contracts; IP registrations and assignments; code licence inventory; employment records; claim papers; privacy policies and vendor contracts; tax filings.

VIIProceedings and forum

Contractual and transactional; disputes under the agreement are handled under its dispute resolution clause.

VIIIHow it could go

  • Findings typically lead to a mix of remediation before closing and contractual protection.
  • Unresolved IP chain issues can affect value significantly.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"Due diligence guarantees there are no risks." It finds what the documents and disclosures reveal within the agreed scope. "Warranties alone are enough." Without remediation or specific indemnities, recovery can be hard. Do not skip contractor IP assignments.

XCommon questions

Who owns code written by contractors?

Often the contractor, unless there is a written assignment.

What happens to risks found in diligence?

They are addressed through price, indemnities, conditions or fixes.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Cross-border carve-outs, listed company takeovers, tax structuring, technical code audits.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

Copyright Act 1957; Companies Act 2013; labour codes and rules; DPDP Act and Rules; FEMA rules; Competition Act 2002; official repository.
