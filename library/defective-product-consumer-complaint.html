Illustrative scenario. This is a hypothetical illustrative scenario prepared for general legal information. It does not describe an actual client matter or guarantee any particular outcome. The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

A fictional consumer buys an appliance with a one-year warranty from a retailer. It fails within months. The retailer redirects to the manufacturer's service centre. The service centre cancels visits and gives no repair date. Written complaints go unanswered.

IIThe practical problem

Getting a repair, replacement or refund, plus compensation for the inconvenience, with minimal cost.

IIIIssues

  1. Whether the buyer is a "consumer" for the purchase.
  2. Defect in goods and deficiency in service.
  3. Which parties to name.
  4. Which Commission has jurisdiction by value and by territory.
  5. Limitation.
  6. Evidence.

IVLegal framework

  • Consumer Protection Act, 2019: consider the definitions of consumer, defect, deficiency and unfair trade practice; applicable remedies and product-liability provisions; mediation provisions; and the two-year limitation period from the cause of action, subject to the Commission's power to condone delay for sufficient cause. Check the precise statutory section and facts.
  • Pecuniary jurisdiction by value of consideration paid, per the 2021 Rules: District up to Rs 50 lakh; State above Rs 50 lakh up to Rs 2 crore; National above Rs 2 crore.
  • Territorial jurisdiction may include the place where the complainant resides or personally works for gain, subject to the Consumer Protection Act and the facts. Check the current statutory wording and whether the complaint is maintainable against each opposite party.
  • Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 where the product was bought online.
  • Confirm the currently operational electronic filing portal, applicable filing procedure, court fee and rules before advising; portal names and processes may change.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Gather invoice, warranty terms, photos or videos of the defect, service tickets and all complaints.
  2. Send a clear written notice to the seller and manufacturer stating the defect, the relief sought and a reasonable deadline.
  3. Consider mediation, which the Act supports where parties agree.
  4. If unresolved, prepare a complaint to the right Commission, with facts, documents, relief and a statement on limitation.
  5. Seek a technical or service report if the defect is technical and disputed.
  6. After an order, follow the enforcement provisions if there is non-compliance.

VIDocuments and evidence

Invoice; warranty card; product details; defect evidence; service history; written complaints with proof of delivery; correspondence; any payment evidence.

VIIProceedings and forum

The District Commission is ordinarily the first-instance forum for claims within its current pecuniary jurisdiction. State and National Commission jurisdiction depends on the Act and Rules; appellate routes and deposit requirements must be separately checked. The illustrative facts do not specify a purchase value, so do not assert a forum until that value is supplied.

VIIIHow it could go

  • Where defect and non-redress are shown, Commissions may order repair, replacement or refund with compensation and costs.
  • Missing documents or delay can weaken the case.
  • Settlement during proceedings is common.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"Only the manufacturer is responsible." The seller can also be liable. "No deadline applies." The limitation period is two years, with limited condonation. Keep every document and message.

XCommon questions

Do I need a lawyer?

Parties may represent themselves, though legal advice can help with drafting and evidence.

Which Commission?

Pecuniary jurisdiction is based on the value of consideration paid for the goods or services, not simply the compensation demanded. Territorial jurisdiction is a separate test. Confirm the current limits and the complainant's facts.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Product liability class actions, housing and builder disputes, insurance claims.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

Consumer Protection Act 2019; 2020 and 2021 Rules; consumer affairs ministry notifications; official repository for authority.
