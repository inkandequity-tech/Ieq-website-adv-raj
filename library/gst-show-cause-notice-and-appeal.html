IHypothetical facts

A fictional trading company receives a show-cause notice alleging it wrongly claimed input tax credit because certain suppliers did not pay tax to the government or did not file returns. The notice covers periods in two financial years: one in FY 2023-24 and one in FY 2024-25. The precise tax periods and alleged conduct must be separated before choosing the statutory provision. The company paid its suppliers by bank transfer and holds tax invoices.

IIThe practical problem

Responding to the notice on time, choosing the right legal provision, and preserving appeal rights, including any pre-deposit.

IIIIssues

  1. Which section the notice should invoke for each period.
  2. Eligibility conditions for input tax credit and the effect of a supplier's default.
  3. Whether the department alleges fraud or suppression.
  4. Time limits for the notice and order.
  5. Appeal and pre-deposit.

IVLegal framework

  • CGST Act, 2017: Sections 73 and 74 generally address determination for periods up to FY 2023-24 (non-fraud and fraud/wilful-misstatement/suppression cases respectively); Section 74A generally applies to determination for FY 2024-25 onwards. Verify the exact period, applicable amendments, notice and order limitation, and statutory exceptions against the current Act and notifications. Do not imply that the supplier's default alone automatically resolves entitlement to ITC.
  • CGST Act Section 16 sets conditions for input tax credit, including possession of prescribed documents, receipt of goods/services, supplier-related conditions, return filing and time limits, as amended. Sections 16(5) and 16(6) introduced retrospective/relief provisions for specified periods and circumstances; assess their exact scope and any relevant circulars for the taxpayer.
  • Appeal to the Appellate Authority under Section 107: the ordinary limitation period and condonable extension are statutory and run from communication of the order. A pre-deposit is generally required. The amount, caps, and special rules for penalty-only orders must be calculated from the currently effective amended Sections 107 and 112 and applicable commencement notifications; do not rely on an unverified date or generic percentage.
  • Appeal to the Appellate Tribunal under Section 112 is subject to separate limitation and pre-deposit requirements. Confirm the relevant Tribunal's operational status, notified benches, filing rules and any transitional directions at the time of filing.
  • State GST Acts, which mirror the central provisions.
  • Writ jurisdiction of the High Court in limited circumstances such as lack of jurisdiction or breach of natural justice.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Read the notice: period, provision invoked, quantum, alleged ground, and the response deadline.
  2. Check whether the provision matches the period (73 or 74 for earlier years; 74A for FY 2024-25 onwards) and whether time limits for the notice are met.
  3. Collect proof of bona fides: invoices, e-way bills, transport records, bank payment proof, GSTR-2B reconciliations, and supplier registration status at the time.
  4. File a reasoned reply on the statutory portal within the stated time, request a personal hearing and attach documents.
  5. If an adverse order follows, calendar the appeal deadline from the date of communication. Check the pre-deposit, and file with the required deposit.
  6. Consider writ remedies only where the circumstances justify bypassing the statutory appeal.
  7. Review internal compliance: supplier due diligence, reconciliation routines and invoice matching.

VIDocuments and evidence

Show-cause notice and order; invoices and e-way bills; payment proofs; transport and delivery records; return filings and reconciliations; supplier registration and filing status.

VIIProceedings and forum

Adjudicating authority; Appellate Authority; Appellate Tribunal; High Court where appropriate.

VIIIHow it could go

  • Strong documentary proof of genuine transactions can help, though courts and authorities apply the statutory conditions strictly.
  • A procedural defect in the notice or order may result in setting aside or remand.
  • Pre-deposit is generally a condition for appeal, so cash-flow planning matters.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"The old section numbers always apply." Later periods fall under Section 74A. "Appeal deadlines are flexible." The Act allows only a limited condonation. Missing the reply or appeal date is usually more damaging than the underlying tax issue.

XCommon questions

Do I have to pay anything to appeal?

Generally a percentage of the disputed amount as pre-deposit, plus any admitted amount; check current rules.

Can I lose credit because my supplier did not pay tax?

ITC depends on the statutory conditions and the applicable period. The effect of a supplier's default is fact-sensitive and should be assessed against the Act, rules, circulars and binding case law; payment to a supplier and possession of an invoice do not by themselves guarantee credit.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Refund claims, classification disputes, anti-profiteering, customs.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

CGST Act 2017 and Rules; State GST Acts; CBIC notifications and circulars; GST portal; official repository for authority.
