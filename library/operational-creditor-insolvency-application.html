Operational Creditor Insolvency Application

When can a supplier or service provider start insolvency proceedings, and what are the risks?

IHypothetical facts

A fictional service provider ("the Creditor") has unpaid invoices from a fictional company ("the Company"). The Company has stopped responding, says some invoices relate to disputed work, and has not paid the undisputed part. The Creditor is considering an insolvency application.

IIThe practical problem

Whether insolvency is the right tool, what must be shown, and what the Company can do in response.

IIIIssues

  1. Whether the debt is an operational debt and above the minimum default threshold.
  2. Whether there is a genuine pre-existing dispute.
  3. The statutory demand notice and the response period.
  4. Limitation.
  5. Consequences of admission and the risk of misuse.
  6. The effect of the 2026 amendments.

IVLegal framework

  • Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC): an operational creditor ordinarily serves a demand notice under Section 8 and may apply under Section 9 after the statutory period if the requirements are met and no qualifying pre-existing dispute is shown. Admission can trigger a collective insolvency process and moratorium. Verify the current consolidated Code, rules and commencement notifications.
  • Minimum default threshold: verify the currently operative notification and any category-specific rule on the date of filing. Do not assume the original statutory amount remains the applicable threshold.
  • Limitation for applications under the Code and the extension of limitation by acknowledgment.
  • Distinguish enactment from commencement. If not verifiable from an official source, remove this detail from public-facing copy.
  • Information-utility requirements: verify whether any proposed or enacted requirement applies to this category of creditor and whether it is in force; do not describe a proposal as a current filing prerequisite.
  • Other routes: civil suit, summary suit, statutory routes for micro and small enterprises.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Establish that the debt is operational, due, above the threshold and within limitation. Gather invoices, delivery proof, and any acknowledgment.
  2. Assess the dispute risk honestly. A real dispute raised before the demand notice is a common reason for rejection.
  3. Consider alternatives: negotiated settlement, civil recovery, statutory remedies. Insolvency has far-reaching consequences and should not be used as ordinary pressure.
  4. If proceeding, serve the statutory demand notice and observe the response period.
  5. If no payment or dispute follows, prepare the application with the required documents and evidence of default.
  6. Debtor side: respond within the statutory period, identify any genuine pre-existing dispute with contemporaneous evidence, consider settlement, and verify the currently effective withdrawal rules before taking a position.

VIDocuments and evidence

Contract and invoices; delivery or performance proof; ledger statements; demand notice and proof of delivery; the Company's replies; bank statements; any prior correspondence on the alleged dispute.

VIIProceedings and forum

National Company Law Tribunal; appeal to the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal; resolution professional and committee of creditors after admission.

VIIIHow it could go

  • Admission is more likely where default is clear and no genuine dispute pre-dates the demand notice.
  • Rejection is likely where a substantial pre-existing dispute is shown.
  • Settlement before admission is common.
  • Frivolous or abusive filings can attract costs or penalties.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"Insolvency is a fast way to get paid." It is a collective process, not an individual recovery tool. "Any unpaid bill qualifies." Thresholds and conditions apply. Record disputes in writing as they arise.

XCommon questions

Can I withdraw after filing?

Withdrawal is governed by the IBC, applicable regulations and judicial orders, and the position may differ before and after constitution of the committee of creditors. Check the current text and applicable approval requirements.

What if the other side says the work was defective?

A genuine, documented dispute can prevent admission.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Financial creditor applications, personal insolvency, cross-border insolvency, liquidation.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

IBC 2016 as amended, including the 2026 Act; IBBI regulations; NCLT rules; commencement notifications; official repository for authority.
