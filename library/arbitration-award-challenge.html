Arbitration Award Challenge

How does arbitration work in India, when can an award be challenged, and how is it enforced?

IHypothetical facts

Two fictional companies sign a supply agreement with an arbitration clause providing for a sole arbitrator and a seat in India. A payment dispute arises. The supplier invokes arbitration and proposes an arbitrator. The buyer objects to the proposed name and later participates under protest. An award is made against the buyer. The buyer considers challenging it, and the supplier wants to enforce.

IIThe practical problem

Both sides need to know how appointment objections are handled, how narrow the challenge grounds are, and how quickly time limits run.

IIIIssues

  1. Is there a valid written arbitration agreement covering the dispute?
  2. Appointment procedure and objections to an arbitrator's eligibility or independence.
  3. Jurisdiction objections before the tribunal.
  4. Time limit for the award.
  5. Grounds and time limit for setting aside.
  6. Enforcement and any stay.
  7. Appeals.

IVLegal framework

  • Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, as amended in 2015, 2019 and 2021: written agreement (Section 7); reference by court (Section 8); interim relief from court (Section 9); appointment of arbitrators (Section 11), including the limited scope of the court's examination at the appointment stage after the 2015 amendment; grounds of challenge to arbitrator and ineligibility (Sections 12 and 14 and the Seventh Schedule), including the issue of unilateral appointment by a party; tribunal's competence to rule on its own jurisdiction (Section 16); interim measures by the tribunal (Section 17); time limit for the award (Section 29A); setting aside (Section 34); enforcement (Section 36); appeals (Section 37).
  • Section 34: a closed list of grounds, including incapacity, invalid agreement, lack of proper notice, matters beyond the scope of submission, improper composition or procedure, non-arbitrability, conflict with the public policy of India, and (for domestic awards) patent illegality; the application must be made within three months of receipt of the award, extendable by up to thirty days on sufficient cause, but not beyond; prior notice to the other party; and the target for disposal within one year.
  • Section 36: enforcement as a decree, with no automatic stay on filing of a Section 34 application since 2015, and a 2021 amendment dealing with unconditional stay where a prima facie case of fraud or corruption is made out.
  • Modification of awards: in Gayatri Balasamy v. ISG Novasoft Technologies Ltd., 2025 INSC 605 (Constitution Bench, 30 April 2025), the Supreme Court recognised limited modification powers in specified circumstances, including severable portions, certain clerical/computational/typographical or manifest errors without merits review, and limited post-award-interest situations; the Supreme Court's Article 142 power is a distinct, carefully constrained matter. Official judgment: https://www.sci.gov.in/sci-get-pdf/?diary_no=207882021&from=latest_judgements_order&order_date=2025-04-30&type=j.
  • Arbitration legislation: check India Code and the Gazette for any enactment or commencement after the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 and its amendments.
  • Commercial Courts Act, 2015: applications under Section 34 in commercial arbitrations lie before the commercial court where the specified value is met.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Read the clause: scope, seat, number of arbitrators, appointment mechanism, rules, institution, and any pre-arbitration steps.
  2. Send a notice invoking arbitration under Section 21 describing the claim and proposing steps for appointment.
  3. If the other side does not agree to an appointment, consider an application under Section 11. If the objection is to independence or eligibility, raise it at the earliest stage and in the statutory manner.
  4. Raise jurisdictional objections before the tribunal at the stage the statute requires.
  5. Plan interim relief, if needed, under Section 9 or Section 17.
  6. After the award, diarise the Section 34 period from the date of receipt. Assess grounds candidly: the challenge is not an appeal on the merits.
  7. For the award holder, prepare enforcement and consider the stay implications.
  8. Consider settlement or mediation at each stage.

VIDocuments and evidence

Agreement and arbitration clause; correspondence invoking arbitration; appointment record; pleadings; evidence and orders; the award and proof of date of receipt; costs and interest computations.

VIIProceedings and forum

Arbitral tribunal; High Court or designated court or institution for Section 11 as per the statute; the court with jurisdiction for Section 9, 34, 36 and 37 applications.

VIIIHow it could go

  • Section 34 is not a merits appeal; the statutory grounds are limited, and courts generally do not reappraise evidence as an appellate court would.
  • Section 34(3) ordinarily allows three months from receipt of the award, with a further period of up to thirty days if sufficient cause is shown, but not thereafter. Verify the applicable computation and any legally relevant Section 33 request or service issue immediately.
  • Following Gayatri Balasamy (2025 INSC 605), limited modification may be permissible in specified circumstances.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"I can appeal the award on the facts." Section 34 is not an appeal. "Filing a challenge stops enforcement." Not automatically. "Late filing can be excused." The statute limits the extension. Diarise the date of receipt on day one, and draft the arbitration clause carefully, including seat, appointment method and institutional rules.

XCommon questions

How long do I have to challenge an award?

The statute sets a short period from receipt of the award, with a limited extension.

Can I choose any arbitrator?

The clause and the statute govern who may be appointed, and certain relationships make a person ineligible.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

International commercial arbitration, foreign awards, emergency arbitration, mediation under the Mediation Act, 2023.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 and the 2015, 2019 and 2021 amendment Acts; the Commercial Courts Act 2015; the Supreme Court and High Court websites for current authority on appointment, unilateral appointment and modification of awards.
