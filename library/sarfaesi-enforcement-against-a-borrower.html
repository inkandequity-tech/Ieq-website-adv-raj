SARFAESI Enforcement Against a Borrower

How can a borrower challenge a bank's enforcement against mortgaged property?

Illustrative scenario. This is a hypothetical illustrative scenario prepared for general legal information. It does not describe an actual client matter or guarantee any particular outcome. The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

A fictional borrower's term loan, secured by a mortgage over a commercial property, becomes a non-performing asset. The lender issues a demand notice, the borrower sends a representation and proposes a one-time settlement, the lender communicates its response, and possession is then taken with assistance from the District Magistrate. Whether the response and possession comply with law depends on the documents and the applicable procedure. A sale notice follows.

IIThe practical problem

Protecting the property from sale, testing the lender's compliance, and exploring settlement or restructuring.

IIIIssues

  1. Whether the loan was properly classified and the notice properly issued.
  2. Whether the lender considered the borrower's representation and gave reasons.
  3. Compliance in taking possession and in conducting the sale.
  4. The remedy before the Tribunal and its time limit.
  5. Pre-deposit for further appeal.
  6. Right to redeem.

IVLegal framework

  • SARFAESI Act, 2002: Section 13(2) generally requires a 60-day demand notice; Section 13(3A) addresses consideration of the borrower's representation and communication of reasons; Section 13(4) lists enforcement measures; Section 14 concerns assistance by the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate/District Magistrate; Section 17 provides an application to the DRT ordinarily within 45 days of the measure challenged; Section 18 governs appeal to the DRAT and generally requires a deposit of 50% of the debt due, reducible by the DRAT for recorded reasons to not less than 25%. Verify the current text and facts.
  • Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993: procedure before the Tribunal.
  • Exclusions and limits on the Act's application, such as certain securities and smaller dues.
  • Interaction with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code: a moratorium in insolvency can restrain enforcement.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Obtain the loan documents, notice, classification letters and all enforcement notices; note receipt dates.
  2. Check whether the notice period, representation process and reasons requirement were followed.
  3. Calendar the Tribunal application deadline from the date of the enforcement measure and prepare the application with grounds, prayer for interim protection and a statement of dues.
  4. Explore settlement or restructuring in parallel, including a one-time settlement, with a written, approved proposal.
  5. Be ready to pay or deposit amounts the Tribunal may require as a condition of interim protection.
  6. Track the sale process urgently.
  7. If the Tribunal's order is adverse, assess appeal and the pre-deposit.

VIDocuments and evidence

Loan agreement and security documents; account statements; classification notice; demand notice and representation; lender's reply; possession and sale notices; valuation reports; settlement correspondence.

VIIProceedings and forum

Debt Recovery Tribunal; Debt Recovery Appellate Tribunal; High Court under Article 226 in narrow circumstances.

VIIIHow it could go

  • Procedural non-compliance can invalidate a step and lead to relief.
  • Tribunals often condition interim protection on a deposit.
  • A negotiated settlement is a common resolution.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"A representation stops the lender." It does not by itself; the lender must consider it. "I can challenge whenever I like." The period to approach the Tribunal is short. Keep every notice and its date of receipt.

XCommon questions

How long do I have to challenge enforcement?

A Section 17 application is ordinarily required within 45 days of the relevant measure under Section 13(4). Identify the exact measure and calculate limitation immediately; do not assume that a representation or settlement discussion stops time.

Can the sale be stopped?

Interim protection depends on the facts, statutory compliance and the Tribunal's or court's order. Filing a challenge does not automatically stay the sale.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Agricultural land and other excluded security, guarantor-specific issues, asset reconstruction company assignments.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

SARFAESI Act 2002 and Rules; RDB Act 1993; RBI circulars on classification and settlement; official repository for authority.
