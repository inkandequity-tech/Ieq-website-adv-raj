Foreign Investment in an Indian Company

What approvals, filings and structuring steps apply to a foreign investor buying into an Indian company?

The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

A fictional foreign fund wants to acquire a majority of the shares of a fictional Indian private company in a sector with foreign investment conditions. Some limited partners of the fund are in a country sharing a land border with India, holding small stakes.

IIThe practical problem

Confirming that the investment is permitted, the route (automatic or government approval), pricing rules, required filings and structuring risks.

IIIIssues

  1. Sectoral caps and conditions.
  2. Land-border-country rules and beneficial ownership.
  3. Pricing and valuation.
  4. Reporting.
  5. Competition law thresholds.
  6. Tax consequences.
  7. Foreign-law issues in the investor's own jurisdiction.

IVLegal framework

  • Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 and the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, with the Consolidated FDI Policy: permitted sectors, caps, automatic and approval routes, pricing guidelines and reporting.
  • Press Note 3 (2020 Series) and DPIIT Press Note 2 (2026 Series), together with any implementing FEMA notification: analyse the complete operative text and definitions of investor, beneficial owner, control, thresholds, sectoral conditions, reporting and prior approval.
  • Sector-specific FDI limits and routes change. Verify the current sectoral policy, applicable press note, FEMA rules/notification and conditions at signing and closing. Any insurance example must be checked against the operative policy and statutory commencement.
  • Companies Act, 2013 on share issue and transfer; Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules for private placements.
  • Competition Act, 2002: combination thresholds, including the deal value threshold.
  • Tax: capital gains, withholding, treaty and anti-avoidance issues; qualified professional review required.
  • Foreign law: the investor's home-law issues must be handled by qualified counsel in that jurisdiction. This practice would not advise on foreign law.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Define the structure: primary issuance, secondary purchase, instruments.
  2. Verify sectoral cap and route. Map the fund's beneficial ownership against the land-border rules and the 10% non-controlling test.
  3. Determine whether combination filing is needed.
  4. Conduct due diligence and structure the transaction documents with conditions precedent for approvals.
  5. Apply pricing and valuation requirements and obtain required valuation reports.
  6. Complete the reporting filings after the transaction within the prescribed time.
  7. Coordinate with tax advisers and foreign counsel.

VIDocuments and evidence

Fund structure chart and beneficial ownership details; shareholding of the target; sector classification; valuation reports; transaction documents; approvals; bank and remittance records.

VIIProceedings and forum

Reserve Bank of India and authorised dealer banks (reporting); the government approval route through the designated authority; Registrar of Companies; Competition Commission of India if applicable.

VIIIHow it could go

  • If the route is automatic and conditions are met, the transaction proceeds with reporting.
  • Where the operative policy requires prior Government approval, the transaction must not close on an assumption that approval can be obtained retrospectively.
  • Errors in route or pricing can lead to contraventions and compounding.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"Any small foreign stake is automatically fine." Beneficial ownership rules matter. "FDI rules are stable." They change often; verify at each step. Document the analysis.

XCommon questions

Do all foreign investments need government approval?

Many are under the automatic route, but sector and investor conditions can require approval.

Can an Indian advocate advise on the investor's home law?

No, that requires qualified counsel in that jurisdiction.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Outward investment by Indian companies, portfolio investment in listed companies, external borrowings.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

FEMA 1999; NDI Rules 2019; Consolidated FDI Policy and press notes on the DPIIT website; RBI master directions; Companies Act 2013; Competition Act 2002.
