Data Protection Readiness for a Retailer

What must a business do under India's data protection law and when?

Illustrative scenario. This is a hypothetical illustrative scenario prepared for general legal information. It does not describe an actual client matter or guarantee any particular outcome. The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

A fictional online retailer collects names, contacts, addresses and purchase histories, and has started using these for marketing messages without a clear notice. A customer asks the retailer to delete their data. The retailer uses third-party vendors for payments and delivery and stores data on cloud servers.

IIThe practical problem

What legal obligations apply now, what will apply later, and how to prepare without overpromising compliance.

IIIIssues

  1. Which law governs today and which obligations are not yet in force.
  2. Notice and consent for marketing.
  3. Handling the customer's deletion request.
  4. Vendor contracts.
  5. Security and breach readiness.
  6. Penalties and governance.

IVLegal framework

  • Until the relevant DPDP provisions commence, other applicable laws and rules may continue to govern relevant processing, including the Information Technology Act, 2000 and the SPDI Rules, 2011 to the extent applicable. Check the DPDP Act's repeal/amendment and commencement provisions, the type of data and processing, and sector-specific obligations; do not assume every pre-existing rule applies to every dataset.
  • Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, which may apply independently.
  • The Act and Rules address notice, consent, certain legitimate uses, data-principal rights, security safeguards, personal-data-breach notifications, children's data and additional obligations for Significant Data Fiduciaries. The exact duty, exceptions, timelines and commencement date differ by provision; confirm each from the official text before describing it as currently mandatory.
  • Penalties under the Act's schedule; the exact amounts and conditions must be read from the Act and not summarised without verification.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Map data: what is collected, why, where it is stored, who receives it and how long it is kept.
  2. Identify which obligations apply today and which will apply from the later commencement dates; build a dated plan.
  3. Revise the privacy notice to describe actual processing; separate marketing consent from order fulfilment.
  4. Create a process for customer requests (access, correction, deletion) with a log and response timelines to be confirmed once the relevant provisions commence.
  5. Review vendor contracts for security, sub-processing, breach cooperation and deletion.
  6. Prepare an incident response plan and internal training.
  7. Review children's data practices if any users may be minors.

VIDocuments and evidence

Data inventory; current privacy notice and consent screens; vendor agreements; security policies; retention schedule; incident log; customer request records.

VIIProceedings and forum

Compliance is primarily internal and regulatory. The Act establishes the Data Protection Board of India; confirm its constitution, operational status, applicable rules and the commencement of the relevant powers before stating which complaints it can currently receive or decide.

VIIIHow it could go

  • A business that maps data, updates notices and tightens vendor contracts will be better placed when obligations commence.
  • Marketing based on data collected for another purpose is a common compliance gap.
  • Compliance timelines can change through further notifications.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"The law is not in force, so I can wait." Some provisions are in force and others commence in stages; preparation takes time. "A policy page is enough." The notice must match actual processing.

XCommon questions

Can a customer demand deletion today under the new Act?

The answer depends on the exact commencement status and any other applicable law, contract or sectoral rule. Check the official enforcement timeline and the facts; do not categorically deny or promise a deletion right.

Does the law apply to small businesses?

Size alone should not be treated as a blanket exemption. Applicability and any exemption depend on the Act, Rules, processing and current commencement status.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Cross-border transfers beyond a general mention, significant data fiduciary audits, sector-specific rules for banking, health, telecom.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

DPDP Act 2023; DPDP Rules 2025; Gazette commencement notifications; MeitY website; IT Act 2000 and rules; E-Commerce Rules 2020.
