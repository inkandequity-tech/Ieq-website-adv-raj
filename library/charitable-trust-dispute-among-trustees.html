Charitable Trust Dispute Among Trustees

What can be done about alleged breach of trust in a charitable trust?

Illustrative scenario. This is a hypothetical illustrative scenario prepared for general legal information. It does not describe an actual client matter or guarantee any particular outcome. The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

A fictional charitable trust runs a school. Two trustees allege that a third trustee, with a relative, arranged to sell a trust property below value and used donations for unrelated expenses. The trust deed requires majority approval for sale of property.

IIThe practical problem

Protecting trust assets, correcting any breach, and avoiding loss of tax registration.

IIIIssues

  1. Whether the trust is private or public and which statute governs it.
  2. Compliance with the deed and approvals.
  3. Duties of trustees and conflicts of interest.
  4. Remedies: accounts, setting aside, removal, recovery.
  5. Limitation.
  6. Tax and registration consequences.

IVLegal framework

  • Indian Trusts Act, 1882 for private trusts and general principles of trusteeship: duties, accounts, breach of trust and removal.
  • For public charitable trusts, the position varies by State: some States have comprehensive public trusts or endowment statutes and charity commissioners; where none applies, Section 92 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 allows suits for breach of public charitable trust with leave of court.
  • Limitation Act, 1963: identify the exact article and nature of relief. Any special rule for suits against express trustees is narrow and should not be generalised to every claim involving a trust or its property.
  • If the body is a society or a Section 8 company, the Societies Registration Act, 1860 as applicable in the State, or the Companies Act, 2013.
  • Income-tax legislation and transitional provisions: identify the statute and provisions applicable to the relevant tax year, registration status, transaction and filing. Verify the Income-tax Act, 2025 and any savings/transitional rules against the official Act and commencement notifications; do not assume that the same Act governs all earlier and later years. review by a qualified tax adviser is mandatory.
  • Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010, if foreign contribution is involved: verify registration/prior permission, permitted receipt and utilisation, bank-account, reporting and restriction requirements on current official sources.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Obtain the trust deed, registration documents, minutes, accounts and property records.
  2. Identify the governing law and regulator.
  3. Demand accounts and explanations; call a trustee meeting with proper notice and record resolutions.
  4. Where appropriate, make a representation to the charity regulator or file the proceeding the State law provides.
  5. If a suit is needed, seek interim orders restraining further alienation, and the remedies of accounts, recovery and removal.
  6. Involve a tax qualified professional to assess registration risk and corrective filings.
  7. Adopt a conflict-of-interest and approval policy.

VIDocuments and evidence

Trust deed; registration certificates; minutes; bank statements; sale documents and valuation; donation records; audit reports; tax filings.

VIIProceedings and forum

State charity commissioner or equivalent where it exists; civil court; High Court; tax authorities for registration issues.

VIIIHow it could go

  • Courts and regulators can restrain dealings with trust property, order accounts and, in serious cases, remove trustees.
  • A transaction below market value may be examined for breach of trust.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"All trusts are governed by the same law." Public and private trusts differ, and States differ. "Majority trustees can do as they wish." Duties and the deed constrain them.

XCommon questions

Can a trustee be removed?

Courts and regulators have power to remove trustees in appropriate cases under the governing law.

Is there a time limit?

Rules differ; some claims concerning trust property have special limitation rules.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Religious endowments, waqf property, FCRA registration, charity tax structuring.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

Indian Trusts Act 1882; relevant State trust or charity law; CPC Section 92; Income-tax Act 2025 and 1961 Act for earlier years; FCRA 2010; official repository.
