Fire Insurance Claim Repudiation

Can an insurer reject a claim for non-disclosure, and what can the policyholder do?

Illustrative scenario. This is a hypothetical illustrative scenario prepared for general legal information. It does not describe an actual client matter or guarantee any particular outcome. The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

A fictional small business has a fire policy over its premises and stock. After a fire, a surveyor assesses the loss. The insurer repudiates the claim, alleging that the business did not disclose an earlier minor fire loss on the proposal form. The business says the earlier incident was disclosed to the agent orally.

IIThe practical problem

Recovering the loss and testing whether the repudiation is legally sound.

IIIIssues

  1. Duty of disclosure and materiality.
  2. Whether the insurer proved non-disclosure and its materiality.
  3. The role and report of the surveyor.
  4. The policy terms and any time limits in the policy.
  5. The correct forum: consumer, civil or arbitration.
  6. The effect of the 2025 amendments.

IVLegal framework

  • Duty of utmost good faith and disclosure of material facts in insurance contracts; Indian Contract Act, 1872 provisions on misrepresentation and fraud; policy terms and proposal form.
  • Insurance Act, 1938. The statutory rule that limits repudiation after a stated period (Section 45) is framed for life insurance; do not assume it applies to a fire policy.
  • Surveyors and loss assessors are governed by the Insurance Act and applicable IRDAI regulations. Verify the currently operative policyholder-protection and claims regulations, their timelines, exceptions and the legal weight of the survey report. Do not imply that the surveyor's assessment automatically binds the insurer or court.
  • Insurance Ombudsman eligibility, subject-matter jurisdiction, monetary limits, exclusions, prior representation requirements and limitation must be checked under the rules in force.
  • Consumer Protection Act, 2019: maintainability must be assessed under the Act and binding case law. Do not assume that a business insurance policy either automatically qualifies or automatically fails the consumer test; analyse the insured, purpose, policy and relief sought.
  • Arbitration clauses in policies and limitation clauses; Limitation Act, 1963 for a suit.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Obtain the policy, proposal form, survey report and the repudiation letter with its reasons.
  2. Reconstruct what was disclosed, to whom and when. Gather evidence of the oral disclosure and the agent's role.
  3. Test materiality: would the earlier loss have affected the insurer's decision to insure or the terms?
  4. Write a detailed representation to the insurer's grievance process, attaching evidence.
  5. Choose the forum with care: civil suit, consumer complaint (if maintainable), arbitration under the policy, or the ombudsman if eligible.
  6. Quantify the loss with documents: accounts, stock records, invoices, repair estimates.
  7. Watch limitation and any policy time limits.

VIDocuments and evidence

Policy and endorsements; proposal form; agent communications; surveyor report; claim form and supporting accounts; repudiation letter; fire brigade or police report; loss evidence.

VIIProceedings and forum

Insurer's grievance cell; Insurance Ombudsman if eligible; civil court; consumer commission if maintainable; arbitrator if the policy requires.

VIIIHow it could go

  • If the insurer fails to prove materiality or the policyholder shows disclosure, the repudiation may be set aside.
  • If material non-disclosure is established, the claim can fail.
  • A negotiated or partial settlement may be possible, depending on the policy, evidence, insurer and applicable procedure; it is not an assumed or typical outcome.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"Insurers cannot repudiate after taking premium." They can in some circumstances. "Telling the agent is always enough." It depends on the facts and the agent's role. Read the proposal form carefully and keep copies.

XCommon questions

Does the three-year rule on challenging policies apply to fire cover?

That rule is framed for life insurance; check the Act before relying on it.

Where do I complain?

It depends on the type of policy and policyholder; there may be several routes.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Life and health insurance claims, motor claims, marine cargo, reinsurance.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

Insurance Act 1938 as amended in 2025; IRDAI regulations; Contract Act 1872; Consumer Protection Act 2019; Insurance Ombudsman rules; official repository.
