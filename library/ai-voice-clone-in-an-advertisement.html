AI Voice Clone in an Advertisement

What rights does a performer have against an AI voice clone used in advertising?

Illustrative scenario. This is a hypothetical illustrative scenario prepared for general legal information. It does not describe an actual client matter or guarantee any particular outcome. The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

A fictional voice performer finds an advertisement featuring a voice that sounds like theirs, produced by an AI tool, endorsing a product they have never worked with. The ad is running on social media and a streaming platform.

IIThe practical problem

Stopping the use quickly, identifying the responsible parties and seeking compensation.

IIIIssues

  1. Whether personality or publicity rights protect the voice and persona.
  2. Passing off and false endorsement.
  3. Performers' rights and moral rights under copyright law.
  4. Advertising and consumer law.
  5. Intermediary takedown.
  6. Evidence and attribution.
  7. Interim relief against unknown persons.

IVLegal framework

  • Personality and publicity rights as developed by High Courts through injunctions in cases involving public figures and misuse of name, image and voice; the scope for non-celebrity performers is less clear.
  • Passing off and false endorsement as part of common law.
  • Copyright Act, 1957: confirm the precise performer-right and moral-right provisions, ownership and duration. Training on or imitating a recording does not by itself resolve infringement; identify the protected subject matter and the relevant act, licence and evidence.
  • Information Technology Act, 2000 and the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, as currently amended: verify the exact intermediary duty, notice/takedown route, deadlines and any operative synthetic-content requirements. Distinguish notified rules from draft amendments or consultation proposals.
  • Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and guidelines on misleading advertisements and endorsements; liability of endorsers and advertisers.
  • Penal provisions on cheating by personation or identity theft online.
  • Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025: a voice recording may be personal data where it relates to an identifiable individual, but applicability depends on the processing and statutory definitions/exemptions. Check the official phased commencement timeline; do not say that all obligations are or are not in force without checking the specific provision.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Preserve evidence: screenshots, URLs, recordings, timestamps, and a comparison with genuine recordings.
  2. Identify the advertiser, agency and platforms. Send notices to platforms using their grievance channels and the legal takedown routes.
  3. Send a cease-and-desist to the advertiser and agency, demanding withdrawal, disclosure of the tool and source data, and an account.
  4. Consider an urgent suit for injunction, including orders against unknown persons where the court allows, and orders directed to intermediaries.
  5. Assess damages and an account of profits.
  6. Prevent recurrence: licensing terms for voice data, contract clauses on AI use, and watch monitoring.

VIDocuments and evidence

Evidence of the advertisement; genuine recordings and contracts; proof of reputation and earnings; takedown correspondence; platform responses; advertiser details.

VIIProceedings and forum

High Court or the civil or commercial court with jurisdiction; platform grievance officers; advertising self-regulation bodies; consumer protection authority for misleading advertisements.

VIIIHow it could go

  • Interim relief may be available on the facts, but the scope of personality rights, especially for non-celebrities and AI-generated imitations, remains fact-sensitive and must be supported by current authority.
  • Platforms may remove content on valid notice.
  • Damages depend on proof of loss and of use.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"There is no law against AI voice clones." Several legal bases may apply, though the law is developing. "Takedown solves everything." It does not compensate. Keep clear contracts on voice use.

XCommon questions

Does a voice have legal protection?

It can be protected through several routes, depending on the facts.

Can the platform be forced to remove the ad?

It can be asked, and courts can direct intermediaries in appropriate cases.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Deepfake sexual content, political deepfakes, training-data copyright claims.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

Copyright Act 1957; IT Act 2000 and the 2021 Rules as amended; Consumer Protection Act 2019; official repository for personality-rights authority.
