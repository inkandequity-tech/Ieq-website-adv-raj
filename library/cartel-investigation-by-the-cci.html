Cartel Investigation by the CCI

What are the risks and options when a competition regulator investigates a suspected cartel?

Illustrative scenario. This is a hypothetical illustrative scenario prepared for general legal information. It does not describe an actual client matter or guarantee any particular outcome. The applicable law and available remedies depend on the facts and jurisdiction; this page is not legal advice.

IHypothetical facts

Three fictional regional distributors of an industrial product exchanged messages about upcoming prices and agreed not to supply a new entrant. A trade body staff member heard of this and a complaint followed. The Competition Commission of India ("CCI") has sent a notice seeking information. The scenario assumes a valid notice; the recipient should check its scope, statutory basis, deadline and any available procedural objections.

IIThe practical problem

Each distributor must decide how to respond, whether to cooperate with the regulator, and how to limit exposure, while preserving privilege and avoiding obstruction.

IIIIssues

  1. Whether the conduct is a horizontal agreement presumed to have an appreciable adverse effect on competition.
  2. Evidence of the agreement and of its implementation.
  3. Leniency.
  4. Penalty calculation.
  5. Settlement and commitment, and their limits.
  6. Search and seizure and privilege.
  7. Appeal.

IVLegal framework

  • Competition Act, 2002, Section 3 (anti-competitive agreements; the presumption for certain horizontal agreements, including price fixing, limiting supply and market allocation; and rule-of-reason analysis for other agreements).
  • Sections 19 and 26 (inquiry, prima facie opinion, investigation by the Director General); Section 27 (orders, including penalties); Section 41 (Director General's powers, including search and seizure).
  • Section 46 and the leniency regulations: reduced penalty for first-in cartel participants who make full and true disclosure; the 2023 amendments added "leniency plus".
  • Penalties: the Amendment Act provides for penalties calculated on global turnover; the CCI's penalty guidelines and turnover regulations govern the method.
  • Appeals to the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (Section 53B).
  • Privilege and confidentiality in competition investigations: verify the applicable statutory provisions and current authority, including whether the communication and the lawyer's role qualify for protection. Do not assume that all in-house legal communications are privileged.

VPossible steps, in sequence

  1. Issue a document preservation notice. Do not delete or alter records.
  2. Engage competition counsel and, separately, consider whether conflicts require separate representation of individuals and the company.
  3. Conduct a privileged internal review of communications to understand the facts.
  4. Evaluate leniency promptly; position in the queue can matter.
  5. Respond to the information request accurately and on time, with careful scope.
  6. Prepare for a possible Director General investigation and for the penalty stage; review financial information relevant to turnover.
  7. Implement a compliance programme and training regardless of outcome.

VIDocuments and evidence

Notice from the CCI; trade body communications; messages and emails among competitors; pricing and supply records; refusal-to-supply correspondence; turnover and financial statements; any compliance policies.

VIIProceedings and forum

Competition Commission of India; Director General; National Company Law Appellate Tribunal; Supreme Court.

VIIIHow it could go

  • If the exchange and the boycott are established, penalties can be significant and are now assessed with reference to turnover as defined in the amended law.
  • A qualifying applicant may be considered for a reduction under the operative leniency framework; eligibility, order of priority and disclosure requirements must be checked against current regulations.
  • Weak evidence of an agreement may lead to closure.

No result is assured.

IXMisconceptions and lessons

"Sharing prices informally is harmless." It can be treated as a cartel facilitator. "We can fix this later." Leniency and cooperation depend on timing. Preserve documents; never destroy records once an inquiry is anticipated.

XCommon questions

Are penalties based only on Indian sales?

The 2023 amendments introduced a global turnover basis; confirm the current method.

XIWhat this scenario does not cover

Abuse of dominance, merger filings, sector regulators.

XIIPrimary sources to consult

Competition Act 2002 and the 2023 Amendment Act; CCI regulations and guidelines; commencement notifications; official repository for authority.
